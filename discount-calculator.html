How to Calculate a Discount

A discount reduces the original selling price of a product or service. Discounts are commonly offered during promotional events, clearance sales, seasonal sales and special customer offers.

Discount Formula

Discount Amount = Original Price × Discount Percentage ÷ 100

Sale Price = Original Price − Discount Amount

Simple Discount Example

A jacket originally costs $120 and is offered at a 25% discount.

How Multiple Discounts Work

When a retailer offers two consecutive discounts, the second discount is normally calculated using the price remaining after the first discount. The percentages should not simply be added together.

Worked Example: Two Discounts and Sales Tax

Suppose a product costs $200. The retailer offers a 25% discount followed by an additional 10% discount. The applicable sales tax is 8% of the discounted price.

Effective Discount Formula

Effective Discount (%) = 100 × [1 − (1 − First Discount ÷ 100) × (1 − Second Discount ÷ 100)]

For example, consecutive discounts of 25% and 10% produce an effective discount of 32.5%, rather than 35%.

Practical Shopping Applications

Comparing Retail Offers

A larger discount percentage does not always mean a lower final price. For example, a $150 product discounted by 40% costs $90, while a $100 product discounted by 20% costs $80. Compare the final prices rather than the advertised percentages.

Percentage Discounts vs. Fixed Coupons

A fixed $20 coupon provides greater savings than a 15% discount on a $100 purchase. However, a 15% discount on a $200 purchase saves $30. The better offer depends on the purchase amount and coupon restrictions.

Understanding Sales Tax

Sales tax can increase the amount you pay at checkout. This calculator assumes tax is applied after all discounts. Actual treatment may depend on local laws, coupon types and retailer policies.

Shopping Within a Budget

Calculate the final price, including taxes, before purchasing. A discounted item may still exceed your budget. Consider whether the purchase is necessary and compare equivalent products from other retailers.

Checking the Original Price

Verify that the advertised original price represents a meaningful comparison. Reviewing historical prices and competing retailers can help determine whether a promotion provides genuine savings.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is 20% off $100?

The discount is $20, resulting in a sale price of $80 before tax.

How much is 30% off $250?

The discount is $75, resulting in a sale price of $175 before tax.

Is 50% off followed by 50% off a free purchase?

No. Two consecutive 50% discounts produce a 75% effective discount. A $100 product would cost $25 before tax.

How do I calculate the original price from a sale price?

Divide the sale price by one minus the discount expressed as a decimal. For example, an $80 sale price after a 20% discount corresponds to an original price of $100. This formula cannot determine the original price after a 100% discount.

Can I use this calculator without sales tax?

Yes. Enter zero in the sales tax field.

Can I calculate only one discount?

Yes. Set the additional discount to zero.

Does selecting another currency convert the price?

No. The currency selector changes the currency displayed. Use our Currency Converter for exchange-rate calculations.
