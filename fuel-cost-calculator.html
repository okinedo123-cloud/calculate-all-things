How to Calculate Fuel Costs

The cost of driving depends primarily on travel distance, vehicle fuel efficiency and the price of fuel. This calculator combines these factors to estimate the fuel expense of your journey.

Fuel Cost Formula Using MPG

Fuel Required = Total Distance in Miles ÷ Miles per Gallon

Fuel Cost = Fuel Required × Price per Gallon

Fuel Cost Formula Using Liters per 100 Kilometers

Fuel Required = Distance in Kilometers × Fuel Consumption ÷ 100

Fuel Cost = Liters Required × Price per Liter

Fuel Cost Formula Using Kilometers per Liter

Fuel Required = Distance in Kilometers ÷ Kilometers per Liter

Fuel Cost = Liters Required × Price per Liter

Factors That Affect Fuel Consumption

  • Driving speed: High speeds can increase fuel consumption.
  • Traffic: Frequent stopping, acceleration and idling can reduce fuel efficiency.
  • Tire pressure: Underinflated tires can increase rolling resistance.
  • Vehicle weight: Heavy loads generally require more energy to transport.
  • Weather: Cold temperatures and air-conditioning use may affect fuel consumption.
  • Road conditions: Hills, rough surfaces and headwinds may increase fuel use.
  • Vehicle maintenance: Proper maintenance can help preserve normal fuel efficiency.

Ways to Reduce Travel Fuel Expenses

  • Combine errands into fewer journeys.
  • Maintain manufacturer-recommended tire pressure.
  • Avoid unnecessary idling.
  • Accelerate and brake smoothly when safe.
  • Remove unnecessary cargo.
  • Compare fuel prices before refueling.
  • Consider carpooling or public transportation.
  • Use realistic fuel economy estimates when planning trips.

Understanding Fuel Measurement Units

Different countries measure vehicle fuel consumption differently. US MPG and Imperial MPG are not interchangeable because their gallon sizes differ.

UnitEquivalent
1 US gallon3.785411784 liters
1 Imperial gallon4.54609 liters
1 mile1.609344 kilometers

When using Imperial MPG, enter the fuel price per Imperial gallon. When using US MPG, enter the price per US gallon. For metric calculations, enter the price per liter.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much gas do I need for a 300-mile trip?

At 25 MPG, a 300-mile trip requires approximately 12 gallons of gasoline.

How do I calculate round-trip fuel costs?

Select Round Trip and enter the one-way distance. The calculator doubles that distance before calculating fuel consumption.

How do I split fuel expenses between travelers?

Enter the total number of travelers, including the driver. The calculator divides the fuel and total journey expenses equally among them.

Can I include tolls and parking?

Yes. Enter the combined additional expense for each complete journey. For a round trip, include the additional expenses for both directions in that amount.

Does the calculator use live gasoline prices?

No. Enter the current or expected fuel price for your location.

Can I calculate diesel fuel costs?

Yes. The same formulas apply to gasoline, diesel and other liquid fuels when the appropriate consumption rate and price are entered.

Does this calculate the full cost of owning a vehicle?

No. It estimates fuel and manually entered additional travel expenses. Vehicle depreciation, insurance, maintenance, financing and registration are not automatically included.

Does changing the measurement system convert my existing inputs?

No. Changing the measurement system resets the distance, efficiency and fuel price to illustrative values. Enter the appropriate values for your vehicle and location.

Does changing currency convert fuel prices?

No. The currency selector changes the displayed monetary unit only. It does not perform currency conversion.
