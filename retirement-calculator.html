How the Retirement Calculator Works

The calculator estimates the future value of your current retirement savings and monthly contributions. It assumes monthly compounding and that contributions are made at the end of each month.

Years Until Retirement

The number of years available for saving can have a major effect on retirement savings. A longer investment period provides more time for contributions and compound growth.

Example

A 40-year-old planning to retire at age 67 has 27 years until retirement. If that person already has $50,000 saved, contributes $500 each month, and earns an average annual return of 7%, the calculator estimates the potential retirement balance at age 67.

Why Monthly Contributions Matter

Regular contributions can substantially increase retirement savings. Even relatively small increases in monthly contributions may have a meaningful effect when maintained over many years.

Expected Investment Return

The expected annual return is only an assumption. Actual investment returns can vary from year to year and may be positive or negative. No investment return is guaranteed.

What This Calculator Does Not Include

This estimate does not account for inflation, taxes, investment fees, employer matching contributions, Social Security benefits, pensions, withdrawals, or changes in contribution amounts unless those amounts are incorporated separately.

Understanding Retirement Savings

Retirement planning involves estimating how much money you may need after you stop working and developing a savings strategy to help reach that goal. Your retirement balance depends on your current savings, contributions, investment returns, and time until retirement.

How Does a Retirement Calculator Work?

A retirement calculator projects the future value of savings using assumptions about investment growth and contributions. Actual results may differ because market returns, inflation, taxes, and expenses change.

Worked Retirement Savings Example

Suppose you begin with $50,000 and contribute $500 at the end of every month for 20 years. Assuming a constant 6% annual return with monthly compounding, your projected balance would be approximately $341,019. Your total contributions, including the initial deposit, would be $170,000. This example excludes taxes and fees.

Why Starting Early Matters

Starting retirement savings earlier allows more time for potential compound growth. Even modest regular contributions can accumulate substantially over decades, although investment returns are not guaranteed.

Inflation and Retirement Purchasing Power

Inflation reduces the future purchasing power of money. For example, at an assumed annual inflation rate of 3%, goods costing $1,000 today would cost approximately $1,806 after 20 years.

Factors Affecting Retirement Readiness

  • Expected retirement age
  • Current savings and monthly contributions
  • Investment returns and market volatility
  • Inflation and healthcare expenses
  • Social Security and other income sources
  • Expected spending and retirement duration

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I save for retirement?

The amount depends on your expected expenses, retirement age, other income, life expectancy, and investment assumptions. No single savings target is appropriate for everyone.

Are retirement projections guaranteed?

No. Projections are estimates based on assumptions. Investment returns fluctuate, and actual results may be higher or lower.

Should inflation be included?

Yes. Considering inflation helps estimate how much purchasing power your future retirement savings may provide.

Can I retire earlier by saving more?

Higher contributions may improve retirement readiness, but retirement timing also depends on expenses, investment performance, other income, and individual circumstances.
