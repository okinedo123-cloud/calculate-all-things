What Is Inflation?

Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services. When prices rise, the same amount of money generally purchases fewer goods and services than before.

For example, if a basket of groceries costs $100 today and prices increase by 3% over the next year, an equivalent basket would cost approximately $103.

How to Calculate Inflation

Inflation compounds over time. Each year's price increase applies to the price level reached during the previous year.

Future Cost Formula

Future Cost = Current Cost × (1 + Annual Inflation Rate)Years

Enter the inflation rate as a decimal when using this formula manually. For example, 3% is represented as 0.03.

Purchasing Power Formula

Future Purchasing Power = Current Amount ÷ (1 + Annual Inflation Rate)Years

This formula estimates the future purchasing power of an unchanged amount of money, expressed in today's prices.

Cumulative Inflation Formula

Cumulative Inflation (%) = [(1 + Annual Inflation Rate)Years − 1] × 100

Practical Applications of Inflation Calculations

Retirement Planning

Inflation can significantly increase future living expenses. If your current annual living expenses are $40,000, a constant inflation rate of 3% would increase equivalent annual expenses to approximately $72,244 after 20 years.

Use our Retirement Calculator alongside inflation projections when evaluating long-term savings goals.

Salary and Wage Adjustments

A salary increase does not necessarily improve purchasing power. If your salary increases by 2% while inflation is 3%, your real purchasing power declines by approximately 0.97%.

Use the Salary Calculator to compare your income across different pay periods.

Inflation and Savings

Money held in an account that earns less than the inflation rate may lose purchasing power, even if the account balance increases.

For example, a savings account earning 2% annually during a year with 3% inflation produces a negative real return before taxes.

Nominal Return vs. Real Return

Nominal return measures the percentage increase in the monetary value of an investment. Real return adjusts that increase for inflation.

Real Return = [(1 + Nominal Return) ÷ (1 + Inflation Rate)] − 1

An investment earning 7% during a year with 3% inflation produces a real return of approximately 3.88%, before taxes and fees.

Planning Future Purchases

Inflation estimates can help you plan for future education costs, household expenses, vehicle purchases and other long-term financial goals. However, individual prices may rise faster or slower than general inflation.

Inflation, Deflation and Disinflation

Inflation: The general price level increases.

Deflation: The general price level decreases. Enter a negative inflation rate to model this scenario.

Disinflation: Inflation slows, but prices may continue rising. For example, a decline in annual inflation from 6% to 3% does not mean that prices have fallen.

How Inflation Is Measured

Consumer price indexes track changes in the prices of selected goods and services over time. In the United States, the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the Consumer Price Index, commonly called CPI.

Individual households may experience different inflation rates depending on their spending patterns, housing costs, healthcare expenses and geographic location.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much will $1,000 be worth in 10 years?

At a constant annual inflation rate of 3%, $1,000 would have purchasing power equivalent to approximately $744.09 in today's money after ten years.

Does 3% annual inflation mean 30% inflation after ten years?

No. Because inflation compounds, 3% annual inflation over ten years produces approximately 34.39% cumulative inflation.

Can inflation be negative?

Yes. Negative inflation is generally called deflation. This calculator supports negative annual inflation rates above minus 100%.

Does this calculator use current official inflation data?

No. It uses the annual inflation rate entered by the user to create a hypothetical projection. It does not automatically retrieve historical CPI data or predict future inflation.

Can I calculate historical inflation?

For an accurate historical comparison, use official price index values for the starting and ending periods. Divide the ending index by the starting index and multiply by the original amount.

Does changing currency change the inflation rate?

No. The currency selector only changes the displayed monetary unit. Enter an inflation assumption appropriate to the economy you are evaluating.
