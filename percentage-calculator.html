Percentage Increase or Decrease

How Percentages Work

A percentage represents a number as a fraction of 100. For example, 25% means 25 out of every 100, which is equivalent to 0.25.

Percentage Formulas

Percentage of a number: (percentage ÷ 100) × number

What percent one number is of another: (part ÷ whole) × 100

Percentage change: ((new value − original value) ÷ |original value|) × 100

Examples

20% of 150 is 30. If 30 is compared with 150, then 30 is 20% of 150. If a value increases from 100 to 125, the percentage increase is 25%.

Common Uses

Percentage calculations are commonly used for discounts, taxes, tips, grades, financial changes, business metrics, statistics and everyday comparisons.

Understanding Percentage Calculations

A percentage expresses a number as a fraction of 100. Percentages are commonly used to calculate discounts, taxes, interest rates, exam scores, and changes in prices.

How to Calculate a Percentage

To find a percentage of a number, multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100.

Example: What is 15% of 200? (15 / 100) × 200 = 30. Therefore, 15% of 200 is 30.

How to Calculate Percentage Increase

Percentage increase = ((New Value - Original Value) / Original Value) × 100.

Example: A product increases from $80 to $100. The increase is $20, representing a 25% increase.

How to Calculate Percentage Decrease

Percentage decrease = ((Original Value - New Value) / Original Value) × 100.

Example: A product decreases from $100 to $75. The decrease is $25, representing a 25% decrease.

Percentage vs. Percentage Points

If an interest rate rises from 4% to 6%, it increases by 2 percentage points. This is also a 50% relative increase compared with the original 4% rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a 20% discount?

Multiply the original price by 0.20 and subtract the result. For a $50 item, the discount is $10 and the final price is $40.

Can a percentage exceed 100%?

Yes. For example, 150% of 200 equals 300. Percentages greater than 100% are common when describing growth or comparisons.

What happens if the original value is zero?

The standard percentage-change formula is undefined when the original value is zero because division by zero is not possible.
